
### **Protocol Addendum: Downtime Projects & Quest Rewards**

Downtime Projects are a core system in *Draw Steel* that allows heroes to pursue long-term, personal goals during the periods of rest (**respites**) between adventures. This system transforms downtime from a simple "health reset" into an active and engaging part of the campaign, driving character development and creating new adventure hooks.

#### **Understanding Downtime Projects: The Mechanics**

A Project is a significant undertaking that requires time, resources, and specific knowledge to complete. Every project is defined by a clear data model.

##### **The Data Model of a Project**

When I design a project, whether it's for crafting a treasure, researching lost lore, or another special activity, I will define it using the following structure:

*   **Project Name:** The clear goal of the project (e.g., *Craft the Blade of Quintessence*, *Discover the Location of the Sunken City*).
*   **Project Type:** The category the project falls into. The main types are:
    *   **Crafting:** Creating a physical item, like a treasure or a vehicle.
    *   **Research:** Uncovering information, translating a text, or finding a location.
    *   **Other:** Special projects like building a stronghold, training an animal, or going undercover.
*   **Item Prerequisite:** A specific, often rare, physical component required to *begin* the project. This is a tangible object the heroes must acquire.
    *   *Example: For the "Build Airship" project, the prerequisite is a "Wind Crystal of Quintessence."*
*   **Project Source:** The "blueprint" or "recipe" for the project. This is the knowledge required to perform the work. It can be a physical text, a knowledgeable tutor (NPC), or even a supernatural vision. The source is often in a specific language.
    *   *Example: For the "Build Airship" project, the source is "Texts or lore in Low Rhyvian."*
*   **Project Roll Characteristic:** The primary characteristic a hero uses when they work on the project (e.g., `Reason`, `Might`, `Intuition`).
*   **Project Goal:** The total number of **Project Points** needed to complete the project. This number reflects the project's complexity and the time investment required.
    *   *Example: A simple consumable might have a goal of 45, while a legendary leveled treasure could have a goal of 450 or more.*

##### **The Process of Completing a Project**

1.  **Starting a Project:** To begin a project, a hero must have met all its prerequisites (possess the Item Prerequisite and have access to the Project Source).
2.  **Working on a Project:** Progress is made during a **respite**. A hero can dedicate their respite activity to work on one of their projects.
3.  **The Project Roll:** The player makes a **Project Roll**, which is a special type of test:
    *   **Roll 2d10 + Project Roll Characteristic + applicable Skill bonus (+2).**
    *   The total of this roll (minimum 1) is converted directly into **Project Points**. There are no tiers of success or failure; every effort contributes something.
    *   **Language Penalty:** If the Project Source is in a language the hero doesn't know, the roll takes a bane (-2 for a related language) or a double bane (-4 for an unrelated language).
4.  **Breakthrough:** If the natural roll on the 2d10 is a 19 or 20, the hero has a **Breakthrough**. They gain the Project Points from their roll and may immediately make a *second* Project Roll for the same project within the same respite activity.
5.  **Completion:** A project is complete when the accumulated Project Points meet or exceed the Project Goal.

#### **Integrating Projects as Quest Rewards**

The true power of the Project system is its ability to turn rewards into compelling, player-driven adventures. Instead of a chest simply containing a finished magic sword, it might contain the key to *making* one. This makes the reward more personal and meaningful.

Here is how I will integrate projects as rewards into the adventures I design for you:

##### **1. The Reward is the Project Source**

This is the most common and versatile project-based reward. The heroes complete a quest and are rewarded with the knowledge to create something amazing.

*   **How it works:** After defeating a dungeon boss, rescuing a sage, or exploring a ruin, the heroes discover the "recipe."
*   **Examples:**
    *   In the library of a defeated necromancer, the party finds the grimoire that serves as the **Project Source** for the *Gravekeeper's Lantern* trinket.
    *   A grateful blacksmith, saved by the heroes, offers to be their tutor, acting as the **Project Source** to teach them the "Imbue Weapon: Chilling I" enhancement.
    *   An ancient map found in a treasure chest is the **Project Source** for the "Discover Lore: Location of the Sunken City" research project.
*   **Effect on the Campaign:** This immediately gives the players a new, tangible goal. They now have the "how," but they still need the "what"—the Item Prerequisite—which leads to the next type of reward.

##### **2. The Reward is the Item Prerequisite**

This reward provides the crucial, rare, component needed for a project. It often serves as a "key" that unlocks a project the heroes may have already known about.

*   **How it works:** The object itself is the primary loot. The quest might have been specifically to acquire this item.
*   **Examples:**
    *   After slaying a young white dragon, the party is able to harvest its heart, which is the **Item Prerequisite** for the *Icemaker Maul*.
    *   A quest to stop a smuggling ring results in the heroes confiscating a "vial of archdevil's blood," the **Item Prerequisite** for the *G'Allios Visiting Card*.
    *   As a reward for saving her, a dryad gives the party a "cutting from an ethereal tree," the **Item Prerequisite** for the *Leyline Walker* armor enhancement.
*   **Effect on the Campaign:** If the heroes already have the Project Source, they can immediately begin crafting. If not, this reward prompts them to seek out the necessary knowledge, launching a new quest.

##### **3. The Reward is a Guide**

A Guide is a special type of reward that accelerates an existing project. It's perfect for speeding up long-term projects or helping a party in a campaign with less downtime.

*   **How it works:** A Guide is a source of information (a book, a tutor, a set of schematics) that grants a large, flat amount of **Project Points** towards a specific project. A hero uses their respite activity to study the guide instead of making a Project Roll.
*   **Examples:**
    *   "You find the personal journal of Stakros the Engineer. Reading it grants you **an immediate 150 Project Points** toward any project to build a siege engine."
    *   "The ghost of the ship's former captain provides a vision of its construction, granting **1,000 Project Points** toward the 'Build Airship' project."
*   **Effect on the Campaign:** This makes epic-scale projects feel more achievable and provides a tangible sense of progress after a difficult adventure.

##### **4. The Reward is Access**

Sometimes, the reward isn't an item, but access to a unique location or person that makes projects easier.

*   **How it works:** By gaining favor with a faction or individual, the heroes are granted access to a special resource.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **A Master Tutor:** An NPC sage or artisan agrees to help the heroes. When a hero works on a project, the Master Tutor can provide an **edge** (+2) to their Project Roll.
    *   **A Unique Workshop/Library:** The party gains access to a location like the Royal Forge or the Grand Library. Any Project Rolls made while using this facility gain a bonus (e.g., +3 to the roll) or are treated as having an automatic **edge**.
*   **Effect on the Campaign:** This provides a persistent benefit and makes a location feel like a valuable, strategic asset for the party.
