Here are detailed instructions on how I will prepare *Draw Steel* adventures for your campaign, with a deep dive into the mechanical details of the negotiation system.

***

### **Protocol: Preparing 'Draw Steel' RPG Adventures**

My objective is to generate comprehensive, mechanically sound, and narratively engaging adventures for your *Draw Steel* campaign. I will adhere to the game's core rules and design principles, focusing on providing you with clear, actionable plans for each session.

#### **Phase I: Campaign & Adventure Scaffolding**

1.  **Establish the Campaign Pitch:**
    *   **Overview:** I will synthesize your core ideas into a concise campaign overview, defining the primary setting, the central conflict, and the types of adventures the heroes will undertake.
    *   **Gameplay Breakdown:** I will assign a High, Medium, or Low rating to the following gameplay categories to guide scene design:
        *   **Combat:** How often will violence be the solution?
        *   **Exploration:** How much of the game involves traversing and discovering new environments?
        *   **Interpersonal:** How frequently will negotiations and social challenges be the focus?
        *   **Intrigue:** How much of the campaign involves mystery, espionage, and political maneuvering?
    *   **Player Buy-In:** I will state the narrative expectations for the player characters. For example: "This campaign requires heroes who are motivated to build a rebellion from the ground up."
    *   **Restrictions:** I will note any restrictions on ancestries, classes, or other options you have defined.

2.  **Outline the Adventure Arc:**
    *   **Goal:** I will define the adventure's primary, actionable goal (e.g., "Stop the cult from completing their ritual," "Rescue the captured heir from the fortress").
    *   **Villain:** I will detail the main antagonist for the adventure, including:
        *   **Motivation & Goal:** What does the villain want and why? (e.g., "Lord Saxton wants to consolidate his power by eliminating rival nobles.")
        *   **Sins:** What evil acts has the villain already committed to establish them as a credible threat?
    *   **Scene Flow:** I will create a flowchart or bulleted list of potential scenes, noting which are critical to the plot and which are optional or reactive to player choices. I will ensure multiple paths can lead to the adventure's goal.

#### **Phase II: Scene & Encounter Design**

I will design a variety of scenes, paying close attention to the mechanical frameworks provided by the game.

##### **A. Combat Encounters**

*   **Objective-Based Design:** Combat will have a clear, narrative objective beyond "kill all enemies," such as "Destroy the ritual altar," "Protect the NPC for 5 rounds," or "Defeat the enemy commander."
*   **Staging:** I will describe the gridded battlefield, noting key terrain, cover, difficult or damaging terrain, and potential environmental interactions.
*   **Monster Roster:** I will select appropriate monsters, creating tactical challenges by combining different monster roles (brutes, controllers, etc.). I will specify initial creature placement.

##### **B. Exploration & Interaction Scenes**

*   **Set the Scene:** I will write a descriptive text for when heroes enter an area, focusing on key sensory details.
*   **Define Discoveries:** I will list the clues, items, or information available.
    *   **Necessary Information:** Information critical to advancing the plot will be discoverable without a test (e.g., it is in plain sight) or will have multiple discovery paths.
    *   **Bonus Rewards:** Extra lore, treasure, or advantages will be hidden behind specific tests.
*   **Define Tests:** For hidden information, I will specify the required test (e.g., "Medium Intuition test using the Search skill to find the hidden compartment").

##### **C. Montage Tests**

*   **Define Goal & Difficulty:** I will state the objective (e.g., "Infiltrate the Palace") and set the difficulty (Easy, Moderate, Hard), which determines the required **Success Limit** and **Failure Limit**.
*   **List Challenges:** I will provide a list of potential challenges and the suggested tests to overcome them (e.g., "Challenge: Bypassing the guards. Test: Agility test using Sneak skill.").
*   **Define Outcomes:** I will write the narrative results for Total Success, Partial Success, and Total Failure, ensuring failure creates a new, interesting problem, not a dead end.

##### **D. Negotiation Encounters (Detailed Mechanical Breakdown)**

This is a critical, mechanically-detailed process that I will follow precisely. The goal is for players to strategically raise an NPC's **Interest** before their **Patience** runs out.

**Preparation:**

1.  **Select/Create the NPC:** I will define the NPC's name, role, and personality.
2.  **Assign Negotiation Stats:**
    *   **Attitude & Starting Stats:** Based on the NPC's attitude (Hostile, Neutral, Friendly, etc.), I will assign starting **Interest** and **Patience** scores from the rulebook's table.
    *   **Impression Score:** I will assign an Impression score based on the NPC's station (e.g., Brigand leader = 1, Monarch = 10).
    *   **Motivations:** I will select at least two motivations (e.g., Greed, Protection). These are the "weaknesses" players should target for easier tests.
    *   **Pitfalls:** I will select at least two pitfalls (e.g., Higher Authority, Revelry). These are conversational "traps" players must avoid.
3.  **Prepare NPC Offers:** For each level of Interest from 2 to 5, I will write a potential offer.
    *   **Interest 5 ("Yes, and..."):** The heroes get what they want, plus a bonus.
    *   **Interest 4 ("Yes."):** The heroes get exactly what they asked for.
    *   **Interest 3 ("Yes, but..."):** The heroes get what they want, but the NPC adds a condition.
    *   **Interest 2 ("No, but..."):** The NPC rejects the request but offers a lesser alternative.
4.  **Plan for Failure (Interest 0):** I will define the negative consequence if the NPC's Interest drops to 0 (e.g., "The NPC becomes hostile and will ambush the party later").

**Running the Negotiation - Player Actions:**

During the negotiation, players can take the following mechanically-supported actions:

*   **Make an Argument:** This is the core action.
    *   The player roleplays their argument and declares which skill they are using (e.g., Persuade, Intimidate, History).
    *   I will determine the appropriate characteristic (Reason, Intuition, Presence) for the power roll (2d10 + characteristic + 2 if a skill applies).
    *   **If the argument appeals to a MOTIVATION (Medium Test):**
        *   **Tier 1 (≤11):** Failure. Patience -1.
        *   **Tier 2 (12-16):** Success with consequence. Interest +1, Patience -1.
        *   **Tier 3 (17+):** Success. Interest +1.
    *   **If the argument is NEUTRAL:**
        *   **Tier 1 (≤11):** Failure with consequence. Interest -1, Patience -1.
        *   **Tier 2 (12-16):** Failure. Patience -1.
        *   **Tier 3 (17+):** Success with consequence. Interest +1, Patience -1.
    *   **If the argument triggers a PITFALL:** Automatic failure. Interest -1, Patience -1. No roll needed.

*   **Discover Motivations/Pitfalls:** A player can use their turn to try and "read" the NPC.
    *   This requires a **Reason, Intuition, or Presence test**.
    *   **Tier 1 (≤11):** They learn nothing and the NPC's Patience drops by 1.
    *   **Tier 2 (12-16):** They learn nothing.
    *   **Tier 3 (17+):** They learn one Motivation or Pitfall of their choice.

*   **Leverage Renown:** If a hero’s Renown is equal to or greater than the NPC's Impression score, they gain an **edge** (+2 bonus) on certain social tests (e.g., a famous hero gets an edge on Persuade tests; an infamous one gets an edge on Intimidate tests).

#### **Phase III: Finalization & Review**

1.  **Integrate Hero Backstories:** I will review the characters' complications, goals, and backgrounds, and weave at least one character-specific element into the adventure.
2.  **Reward Structure:** I will list the potential rewards: Victories (which convert to XP), Titles, Renown increases, Wealth, and specific Treasures (Consumables, Trinkets, and Leveled Treasures), ensuring they are appropriate for the party's echelon.
3.  **Final Review:** I will conduct a final review of the entire adventure plan to check for logical consistency and ensure all encounters are prepared with the necessary mechanical details.
