### **Protocol: Preparing 'Draw Steel' RPG Adventures**

My objective is to generate comprehensive, mechanically sound, and narratively engaging adventures for your *Draw Steel* campaign. I will adhere to the game's core rules and design principles, focusing on providing you with clear, actionable plans for each session.

#### **Phase I: Campaign & Adventure Scaffolding**

1.  **Establish the Campaign Pitch:**
    *   **Overview:** I will synthesize your core ideas into a concise campaign overview, defining the primary setting, the central conflict, and the types of adventures the heroes will undertake.
    *   **Gameplay Breakdown:** I will assign a High, Medium, or Low rating to the following gameplay categories to guide scene design:
        *   **Combat:** How often will violence be the solution?
        *   **Exploration:** How much of the game involves traversing and discovering new environments?
        *   **Interpersonal:** How frequently will negotiations and social challenges be the focus?
        *   **Intrigue:** How much of the campaign involves mystery, espionage, and political maneuvering?
    *   **Player Buy-In:** I will state the narrative expectations for the player characters. For example: "This campaign requires heroes who are motivated to build a rebellion from the ground up."
    *   **Restrictions:** I will note any restrictions on ancestries, classes, or other options you have defined.

2.  **Outline the Adventure Arc:**
    *   **Goal:** I will define the adventure's primary, actionable goal (e.g., "Stop the cult from completing their ritual," "Rescue the captured heir from the fortress").
    *   **Villain:** I will detail the main antagonist for the adventure, including:
        *   **Motivation & Goal:** What does the villain want and why? (e.g., "Lord Saxton wants to consolidate his power by eliminating rival nobles.")
        *   **Sins:** What evil acts has the villain already committed to establish them as a credible threat?
    *   **Scene Flow:** I will create a flowchart or bulleted list of potential scenes, noting which are critical to the plot and which are optional or reactive to player choices. I will ensure multiple paths can lead to the adventure's goal.

#### **Phase II: Scene & Encounter Design**

I will design a variety of scenes, paying close attention to the mechanical frameworks provided by the game.

##### **A. Combat Encounters**

*   **Objective-Based Design:** Combat will have a clear, narrative objective beyond "kill all enemies," such as "Destroy the ritual altar," "Protect the NPC for 5 rounds," or "Defeat the enemy commander."
*   **Staging:** I will describe the gridded battlefield, noting key terrain, cover, difficult or damaging terrain, and potential environmental interactions.
*   **Monster Roster:** I will select appropriate monsters, creating tactical challenges by combining different monster roles (brutes, controllers, etc.). I will specify initial creature placement.

##### **B. Exploration & Interaction Scenes**

*   **Set the Scene:** I will write a descriptive text for when heroes enter an area, focusing on key sensory details.
*   **Define Discoveries:** I will list the clues, items, or information available.
    *   **Necessary Information:** Information critical to advancing the plot will be discoverable without a test (e.g., it is in plain sight) or will have multiple discovery paths.
    *   **Bonus Rewards:** Extra lore, treasure, or advantages will be hidden behind specific tests.
*   **Define Tests:** For hidden information, I will specify the required test (e.g., "Medium Intuition test using the Search skill to find the hidden compartment").

##### **C. Montage Tests**

*   **Define Goal & Difficulty:** I will state the objective (e.g., "Infiltrate the Palace") and set the difficulty (Easy, Moderate, Hard), which determines the required **Success Limit** and **Failure Limit**.
*   **List Challenges:** I will provide a list of potential challenges and the suggested tests to overcome them (e.g., "Challenge: Bypassing the guards. Test: Agility test using Sneak skill.").
*   **Define Outcomes:** I will write the narrative results for Total Success, Partial Success, and Total Failure, ensuring failure creates a new, interesting problem, not a dead end.

##### **D. Negotiation Encounters (Detailed Mechanical Breakdown)**

This is a critical, mechanically-detailed process that I will follow precisely. The goal is for players to strategically raise an NPC's **Interest** before their **Patience** runs out.

**Preparation:**

1.  **Select/Create the NPC:** I will define the NPC's name, role, and personality.
2.  **Assign Negotiation Stats:**
    *   **Attitude & Starting Stats:** Based on the NPC's attitude (Hostile, Neutral, Friendly, etc.), I will assign starting **Interest** and **Patience** scores from the rulebook's table.
    *   **Impression Score:** I will assign an Impression score based on the NPC's station (e.g., Brigand leader = 1, Monarch = 10).
    *   **Motivations:** I will select at least two motivations (e.g., Greed, Protection). These are the "weaknesses" players should target for easier tests.
    *   **Pitfalls:** I will select at least two pitfalls (e.g., Higher Authority, Revelry). These are conversational "traps" players must avoid.
3.  **Prepare NPC Offers:** For each level of Interest from 2 to 5, I will write a potential offer.
    *   **Interest 5 ("Yes, and..."):** The heroes get what they want, plus a bonus.
    *   **Interest 4 ("Yes."):** The heroes get exactly what they asked for.
    *   **Interest 3 ("Yes, but..."):** The heroes get what they want, but the NPC adds a condition.
    *   **Interest 2 ("No, but..."):** The NPC rejects the request but offers a lesser alternative.
4.  **Plan for Failure (Interest 0):** I will define the negative consequence if the NPC's Interest drops to 0 (e.g., "The NPC becomes hostile and will ambush the party later").

**Running the Negotiation - Player Actions:**

During the negotiation, players can take the following mechanically-supported actions:

*   **Make an Argument:** This is the core action.
    *   The player roleplays their argument and declares which skill they are using (e.g., Persuade, Intimidate, History).
    *   I will determine the appropriate characteristic (Reason, Intuition, Presence) for the power roll (2d10 + characteristic + 2 if a skill applies).
    *   **If the argument appeals to a MOTIVATION (Medium Test):**
        *   **Tier 1 (≤11):** Failure. Patience -1.
        *   **Tier 2 (12-16):** Success with consequence. Interest +1, Patience -1.
        *   **Tier 3 (17+):** Success. Interest +1.
    *   **If the argument is NEUTRAL:**
        *   **Tier 1 (≤11):** Failure with consequence. Interest -1, Patience -1.
        *   **Tier 2 (12-16):** Failure. Patience -1.
        *   **Tier 3 (17+):** Success with consequence. Interest +1, Patience -1.
    *   **If the argument triggers a PITFALL:** Automatic failure. Interest -1, Patience -1. No roll needed.

*   **Discover Motivations/Pitfalls:** A player can use their turn to try and "read" the NPC.
    *   This requires a **Reason, Intuition, or Presence test**.
    *   **Tier 1 (≤11):** They learn nothing and the NPC's Patience drops by 1.
    *   **Tier 2 (12-16):** They learn nothing.
    *   **Tier 3 (17+):** They learn one Motivation or Pitfall of their choice.

*   **Leverage Renown:** If a hero’s Renown is equal to or greater than the NPC's Impression score, they gain an **edge** (+2 bonus) on certain social tests (e.g., a famous hero gets an edge on Persuade tests; an infamous one gets an edge on Intimidate tests).

##### **E. Montage Tests (Detailed Mechanical Breakdown)**

I will use the Montage Test system to resolve complex, multi-stage group challenges that occur over a period of time. This framework is ideal for cinematic sequences like chases, long-distance travel through dangerous lands, preparing a village for a siege, or infiltrating a large, complex location. It turns a potentially long and unstructured narrative segment into a tactical, team-based challenge.

**Preparation:**

1.  **Define the Goal and Stakes:** I will clearly state what the heroes are trying to accomplish and what the consequences of failure are.
    *   *Example Goal:* Cross the Blighted Swamp to reach the Sunken Temple.
    *   *Example Stakes:* You must arrive within three days to stop the cult's ritual. Failure means the cult summons a powerful demon.

2.  **Set the Difficulty & Limits:** The difficulty of the montage determines the mechanical targets for the players. For a party of four to five heroes over two rounds, I will use the following baseline:
    *   **Easy:** Success Limit: 5, Failure Limit: 5
    *   **Moderate:** Success Limit: 6, Failure Limit: 4
    *   **Hard:** Success Limit: 7, Failure Limit: 3
    *   I will adjust these limits up or down by 1 for each hero above or below a party size of five to maintain balance.

3.  **List the Challenges:** I will create a list of distinct obstacles the heroes must overcome. This list provides a menu of options for the players to choose from when deciding their actions. Each challenge will have suggested characteristics and skills for the test.
    *   *Example Challenges for the Blighted Swamp:*
        *   **Navigating the Murky Paths:** Finding a safe route through the confusing, half-submerged trails. *Suggested Test:* Intuition test using Navigate or Nature skills.
        *   **Fording the Fetid River:** Crossing a deep, fast-moving river filled with debris and leeches. *Suggested Test:* Might test using Swim or Endurance skills.
        *   **Enduring the Toxic Fumes:** Resisting the sickening miasma rising from the swamp that saps strength. *Suggested Test:* Might test using the Endurance skill.
        *   **Avoiding Hostile Fauna:** Sneaking past giant venomous snakes and hungry swamp gators. *Suggested Test:* Agility test using Sneak or Intuition test using Nature skill.
        *   **Clearing Tangled Overgrowth:** Hacking through thick, thorny vines that block the way. *Suggested Test:* Might test using Endurance or Lift skills.

4.  **Plan for Twists (Optional):** To increase tension, I may script a twist that occurs after the first montage round, pausing the test for an immediate challenge, such as a combat encounter or a sudden hazard.

5.  **Define the Outcomes:** I will write clear narrative descriptions for the three possible outcomes.
    *   **Total Success:** The heroes achieve their goal efficiently and effectively, often with a minor advantage. *(Example: You arrive at the Sunken Temple a day early, giving you time to rest and scout the location before the ritual begins.)*
    *   **Partial Success:** The heroes achieve their goal, but at a cost or with a complication. *(Example: You arrive at the Sunken Temple just as the final incantations of the ritual are being spoken, forcing you into immediate action with no time to prepare.)*
    *   **Total Failure:** The heroes fail to achieve their primary objective, leading to a new, more difficult problem. *(Example: The swamp proves too treacherous, forcing you to turn back. The ritual is completed, and now you must face the summoned demon as it lays waste to the surrounding lands.)*

**Running the Montage Test - Player Actions:**

The test proceeds in **Montage Rounds**. A montage is typically limited to two rounds. In each round, every player character must take one of the following actions. A player must declare their action and the skill they are using.

*   **Make a Test:** This is the primary action. The player describes how their hero tackles one of the listed challenges (or another plausible challenge they invent). They then make the appropriate power roll.
    *   The difficulty of each *individual test* within the montage is set by me (Easy, Medium, or Hard).
    *   The result of their roll (Success, Failure, with or without Consequence/Reward) is narrated.
    *   One **Success** or **Failure** is added to the montage's running total.
    *   **A character cannot use the same skill twice** during a single montage test.

*   **Assist a Test:** A player can choose to assist another hero's test.
    *   The assisting character must use a skill that is different from the one the primary character is using. The player must justify how their skill helps. (e.g., "While Jorn makes a Might test with the Climb skill to scale the cliff, I'll use my knowledge of Architecture to point out the most stable handholds.")
    *   The assisting character makes an **assist test**.
    *   **Tier 1 (≤11):** The primary character takes a **bane** on their test.
    *   **Tier 2 (12-16):** The primary character gains an **edge** (+2) on their test.
    *   **Tier 3 (17+):** The primary character gains a **double edge** (+4) on their test.
    *   The assisting character's action counts as their action for the round. The primary character's test provides the Success or Failure for the montage.

*   **Use an Ability or Item:** A player can describe using a class feature, treasure, or other ability to overcome a challenge. I will adjudicate the result. A sufficiently powerful or clever use might grant an **automatic success** for the montage, or perhaps provide an edge to all other heroes for the rest of the round.

**Ending the Montage & Determining the Outcome:**

The montage test ends when one of three conditions is met:
1.  The number of successes reaches the **Success Limit**.
2.  The number of failures reaches the **Failure Limit**.
3.  The final hero has acted in the second montage round.

I will then compare the final totals to determine the outcome:
*   **Total Success:** The Success Limit was reached before the Failure Limit or the end of the second round.
*   **Partial Success:** The Failure Limit was reached OR the second round ended, AND the heroes have at least two more successes than failures.
*   **Total Failure:** The Failure Limit was reached OR the second round ended, AND the heroes do NOT have at least two more successes than failures.

Finally, I will narrate the pre-written outcome that corresponds to the result, transitioning the story into the next scene.

Phase III: Finalization & Review**

1.  **Integrate Hero Backstories:** I will review the characters' complications, goals, and backgrounds, and weave at least one character-specific element into the adventure.
2.  **Reward Structure:** I will list the potential rewards: Victories (which convert to XP), Titles, Renown increases, Wealth, and specific Treasures (Consumables, Trinkets, and Leveled Treasures), ensuring they are appropriate for the party's echelon.
3.  **Final Review:** I will conduct a final review of the entire adventure plan to check for logical consistency and ensure all encounters are prepared with the necessary mechanical details.


### **Draw Steel: Combat Rules Summary**

This document outlines the core mechanics for running combat encounters in the Draw Steel roleplaying game.

#### **The Combat Round**

Combat is structured in rounds, with each creature taking one turn per round.

- **Initiative:** Draw Steel uses a side-based initiative system. At the start of combat (after determining surprise), one side is determined to go first (usually via a d10 roll). Turns then alternate between the heroes' side and the Director's side. The players decide amongst themselves which hero acts when it is their side's turn.
    
- **Surprise:** Creatures who are unaware of their enemies at the start of combat are **surprised**. A surprised creature cannot take triggered actions, and ability rolls made against them gain an **edge**. Surprise ends at the end of the first combat round.
    

#### **Actions on a Turn**

On your turn, you can perform one of each of the following actions in any order. You can break up your movement between your other actions.

- **Main Action:** Your most significant action. This is typically used to activate a powerful class ability or make a concerted attack.
    
- **Maneuver:** A smaller, quicker action. This includes things like grabbing an enemy, aiding an ally, or using certain class features.
    
- **Move Action:** Used to move around the battlefield.
    
- **Action Flexibility:** You can choose to use your Main Action to instead perform an additional Maneuver or an additional Move Action.
    

- **Triggered Action:** An action used in response to a specific trigger (e.g., "when an enemy deals damage to you"). You can only use **one** triggered action per round.
    
- **Free Triggered Action:** Functions like a triggered action, but does not count against your one-per-round limit.
    
- **Free Maneuver:** A very simple action that doesn't cost a maneuver, such as drawing a weapon or opening an unlocked door. You can perform these freely on your turn.
    
- **No Action:** The simplest activities, like shouting a brief warning or dropping an item, which can be done at any time.
    

#### **Movement Rules**

- **Speed:** Your speed is the number of squares you can move when you take the Advance move action.
    
- **Moving Through Spaces:** You can move freely through an ally's space. An enemy's space is considered **difficult terrain** (costs 1 additional square of movement to enter). You cannot end your move in another creature's space unless they are two or more sizes larger or smaller than you.
    
- **Shifting:** A careful, deliberate movement that does **not** provoke opportunity attacks. Certain abilities allow you to shift. You cannot shift into or out of difficult terrain.
    
- **Climbing & Swimming:** Unless you have a feature that allows you to do so automatically, it costs 2 squares of movement to climb or swim 1 square. A test may be required for difficult surfaces or rough water.
    
- **Flying:** Allows movement through the air. If a flying creature is knocked prone or has its speed reduced to 0, it falls. Creatures with the **hover** ability ignore this.
    
- **Jumping:** As part of a move, you can automatically long jump a number of squares up to your Might or Agility score. A test is required to jump farther or higher.
    
- **Teleporting:** Instantaneous movement that bypasses obstacles and does not provoke opportunity attacks. You must have line of effect to the destination space. Teleporting ends the **grabbed** and **restrained** conditions.
    

#### **Falling Rules**

- **Damage:** A creature takes **2 damage for each square they fall** after the first, up to a maximum of 50 damage. They land **prone**.
    
- **Reducing Damage:** A creature can reduce the effective distance of the fall by a number of squares equal to their **Agility score**.
    
- **Falling on a Creature:** If a creature falls onto another, both creatures take the fall damage. The falling creature lands prone in the nearest unoccupied space. If the falling creature's size is greater than the landed-upon creature's Might score, the landed-upon creature is also knocked prone.
    

#### **Key Combat Concepts**

- **Stamina:** This represents a creature's health and vitality. Damage reduces Stamina.
    
- **Winded:** A creature is winded when their Stamina is at or below half of their maximum. This state can trigger certain abilities.
    
- **Dying:** When a creature's Stamina is 0 or lower, they are **dying**. A dying creature can still act but is **bleeding** and cannot use the Catch Breath maneuver. They die if their Stamina reaches a negative value equal to their winded value.
    
- **Opportunity Attack:** If an adjacent enemy **willingly moves away** from you without shifting, you can use a free triggered action to make a **melee free strike** against them.
    
- **Flanking:** When you and an ally are on opposite sides of an enemy, you are flanking them. You gain an **edge** on melee strikes against that enemy.
    
- **Cover:** A physical obstacle blocking at least half of a target. Damage-dealing abilities used against a target with cover take a **bane**.
    
- **Concealment:** Visual obstruction (fog, darkness, invisibility). Strikes against a target with concealment take a **bane**.

### **Conditions Summary**

- **Bleeding:** Takes damage (1d6 + level) when using a main action, triggered action, or making a Might/Agility roll.
    
- **Dazed:** Limited to one action type per turn (main, maneuver, or move). Cannot use triggered actions.
    
- **Frightened:** Takes a bane on ability rolls against the source of fear and cannot willingly move closer to it.
    
- **Grabbed:** Speed is 0. Takes a bane on abilities that don't target the grabbing creature/effect.
    
- **Prone:** Lying on the ground. Takes a bane on their own strikes. Melee abilities against them gain an edge. Movement costs double.
    
- **Restrained:** Speed is 0 and cannot be force moved or stand up. Takes a bane on ability rolls and Might/Agility tests. Abilities used against them gain an edge.
    
- **Slowed:** Speed becomes 2. Cannot shift.
    
- **Taunted:** Has a double bane on ability rolls that do not target the creature/effect that taunted them.
    
- **Weakened:** Takes a bane on all power rolls.


#### **HOUSERULE! Falling Object Trajectory & Deviation**

This rule determines where a falling object lands.

- **Deviation Calculation:**
    
    1. Determine the **Impact Point** (the square directly below).
        
    2. For every **5 full squares** the object falls, it gains **1 Deviation Point**.
        
    3. Roll a **d10** and consult the **Scatter Diagram** to find the direction of deviation.
        
    4. The object's final landing square is a number of squares away from the Impact Point equal to the Deviation Points, in the direction rolled.
        
- **Scatter Diagram:**
    
    - **1:** Up-Left | **2:** Up | **3:** Up-Right | **4:** Left | **5-6:** No Deviation | **7:** Right | **8:** Down-Left | **9:** Down | **10:** Down-Right
        

#### **HOUSERULE! Sliding and Rolling Objects on Slopes**

This rule determines how an object moves after landing on a sloped surface.

1. **Gain Momentum:** The object gains **1 Momentum Point for every 2 full squares it fell**.
    
2. **Determine Slide Distance:** The base Slide Distance is equal to its Momentum Points. This is modified by object type (rollers move farther) and slope steepness (steeper slopes double the distance).
    
3. **Resolve Movement:** The object moves down the slope, spending its Slide Distance. Difficult terrain costs double. The movement stops when Slide Distance is 0 or it collides with a significant obstacle.
    
4. **Collision Damage:** If it hits a creature, the creature takes **1 damage for every point of Slide Distance remaining**.
    

#### **HOUSERULE! Structural Integrity & Breaking Points**

This framework governs attempts to move or topple objects that are **attached** to the ground or another larger structure (e.g., a tree, a stone pillar, a statue on a plinth). These objects cannot be force moved normally.

- **System Activation:** This system activates whenever an ability applies a push or pull to an Attached Object. A slide cannot sunder an attached object; the forced movement simply fails.
    
- **Defining an Attached Object:** An Attached Object is any object that is rooted to the ground or is part of a larger structure. The Director determines if an object qualifies.
    
- **Breaking Point:** Every Attached Object has a **Breaking Point**, which is a value representing the force needed to snap it from its anchor. The Breaking Point is equal to the object's **Stamina**.
    
    - Example: A 1-square thick wooden tree (Size 1M) has 3 Stamina, so its Breaking Point is 3.
        
    - Example: A 1-square thick stone pillar (Size 1M) has 6 Stamina, so its Breaking Point is 6.
        

When you use an ability that applies a push X or pull X to an Attached Object, follow these steps:

1. **Determine Force Value:** The number of squares of the push or pull is the **Force Value**. (e.g., a push 10 has a Force Value of 10).
    
2. **Deal Initial Damage:** The object takes the normal damage from your ability, if any.
    
3. **Compare Force to Breaking Point:**
    
    - **If Force Value is LESS than the Breaking Point:** The object is **not sundered**.
        
        - It does not move.
            
        - The object takes additional damage equal to the **Force Value**, representing the strain on its structure. Its Stamina (and thus its Breaking Point) is reduced for future attempts.
            
    - **If Force Value is EQUAL TO OR GREATER than the Breaking Point:** The object is **sundered**. It breaks free from its anchor point. Now, determine its final movement:
        
        - **For Movers (e.g., Trees, large crystals):** The object is uprooted and is now pushed or pulled. The distance it travels is equal to the **original forced movement value MINUS the object's Size**. It then moves as a normal free object.
            
        - **For Topplers (e.g., Pillars, Statues):** The object does not get pushed or pulled horizontally. Instead, it **falls**. The character who sundered it chooses the direction it falls. It topples in a straight line, occupying a number of squares equal to its height. Any creature in those squares is subject to the **Falling Object Damage** rules. The "Squares Fallen" for the damage calculation is **half the object's height** (rounded down).
            

**Example Application:**

- **Pushing a Tree:** A hero uses an ability that applies push 10 to a Size 1M tree (Breaking Point 3). The Force Value is 10. Since 10 > 3, the tree is **sundered**. It is now pushed a distance of 10 (from the ability) - 1 (the tree's size) = **9 squares**.
    
- **Toppling a Pillar:** A hero uses an ability that applies push 10 to a 10-square-high stone pillar (Size 1M, Breaking Point 6). The Force Value is 10. Since 10 > 6, the pillar is **sundered**. The hero decides it will fall north. It topples, covering the 10 squares to the north. Any creature in its path takes falling object damage as if it fell 5 squares: **(2 x 5) x 1 = 10 damage**.
    
- **Failed Push:** A hero uses an ability that applies push 4 to the same 10-square-high stone pillar (Breaking Point 6). The Force Value is 4. Since 4 < 6, the pillar is **not sundered**. It does not move or fall. It takes damage from the initial ability, plus an additional 4 damage from the failed push. Its Stamina is now reduced, making it easier to break on the next attempt.
    

#### **HOUSERULE! Pinning**

This rule creates a new **Pinned** condition for when a creature is trapped beneath a significantly larger creature or object.

- **Triggering Pinning:** A creature becomes pinned when a creature or object **two or more sizes larger** than them ends its movement occupying their space.
    
- **The Pinned Condition:** A pinned creature is **restrained**, **prone**, has **no line of effect** to creatures not adjacent to them, and all creatures have **cover** against them.
    
- **Escaping a Pin:** A pinned creature or their allies can free them using standard game actions:
    
    - **Force:** Use the **Knockback maneuver** on the pinning creature/object to push it off.
        
    - **Destruction:** Use a **main action** to make a strike against a pinning object, attempting to destroy one square of it to create an opening.
        
    - **Wriggling Free:** The pinned creature can use their **move action** to make a **Hard Agility test** to crawl out from underneath. Relevant skills (e.g., Gymnastics) can be applied.

### **Foundational Concept: Echelons of Play**

Before diving into items and titles, it's crucial to understand Echelons. Echelons are the four distinct tiers of power that define a hero's journey in *Draw Steel*. They represent not just the character's level, but the scope and scale of the challenges they face and the rewards they can earn. When I design adventures, I will build them around the party's current Echelon.

*   **1st Echelon (Levels 1-3): Local Heroes**
    *   **Scope:** Saving a single village, a city district, or a small barony.
    *   **Threats:** Bandits, goblins, ogres, lesser undead, and corrupt local officials.
    *   **Rewards:** Heroes acquire their first useful treasures and might earn a local title like "Brawler" or "Local Hero."

*   **2nd Echelon (Levels 4-6): Regional Saviors**
    *   **Scope:** Defending an entire kingdom, leading a small army, thwarting a major villainous organization.
    *   **Threats:** Giants, medusas, powerful devils, and organized military forces led by skilled commanders.
    *   **Rewards:** Heroes find or craft more powerful leveled treasures and can earn significant titles like "Knight" or "Battlefield Commander."

*   **3rd Echelon (Levels 7-9): World Savers**
    *   **Scope:** Confronting threats to an entire continent or world, dealing with extraplanar incursions.
    *   **Threats:** Vampires, powerful dragons, valok, and lieutenants of world-ending villains.
    *   **Rewards:** Leveled treasures are fully unlocked, and titles like "Demon Slayer" or "Peace Bringer" reflect their world-shaping deeds.

*   **4th Echelon (Level 10): Legends of the Timescape**
    *   **Scope:** Battling demigods, saving the multiverse (the Timescape), confronting the ultimate campaign villain.
    *   **Threats:** Liches, ancient dragons, archdevils, and universe-threatening entities like Ajax the Invincible.
    *   **Rewards:** Heroes may acquire artifacts and earn legendary titles like "Monarch" or "Demigod."

### **Understanding Items (Treasures)**

In *Draw Steel*, items with supernatural properties are called **Treasures**. They are rare, powerful, and central to a hero's progression. They are not typically purchased in shops but are found in dangerous locations, received as rewards from powerful NPCs, or crafted through significant effort during downtime.

There are four primary types of Treasures:

1.  **Consumables:** Single-use items like potions, scrolls, or alchemical bombs.
2.  **Trinkets:** Items with persistent, often utility-focused magical properties, like gloves that help you climb or a cloak that aids in stealth.
3.  **Leveled Treasures:** The most significant equipment. These are typically armor, weapons, and implements that grow in power as the hero gains levels.
4.  **Artifacts:** Unique, world-changing items with immense power that can define an entire campaign.

#### **The Data Model of an Item**

When I create or present a treasure for your campaign, I will use the following data structure:

*   **Name:** The item's evocative name (e.g., *Blade of Quintessence*).
*   **Flavor Text:** A short, descriptive sentence to set the mood.
*   **Keywords:** Tags that define the item's mechanics and type.
    *   **Supernatural Type:** `Magic` or `Psionic`.
    *   **Item Category:** `Potion`, `Light Armor`, `Heavy Weapon`, `Implement`, `Ring`, etc. This determines which kits or character types can use it effectively.
    *   **Body Slot (for wearables):** `Head`, `Neck`, `Arms`, `Hands`, `Waist`, `Feet`.
*   **Item Prerequisite (for crafting):** The rare, narrative component needed to begin crafting the item (e.g., "A ruby hardened in the fires of the City of Brass"). This serves as an adventure hook.
*   **Project Source (for crafting):** The "recipe" or knowledge needed, often in a specific language (e.g., "Texts or lore in Zaliac").
*   **Project Roll Characteristic (for crafting):** The primary characteristic used for the crafting project roll (e.g., `Might`, `Reason`, or `Intuition`).
*   **Project Goal (for crafting):** The number of project points required to complete the item.
*   **Effect / Leveled Benefits:** This is the core mechanical description.
    *   For **Consumables** and **Trinkets**, this is a single "Effect" block.
    *   For **Leveled Treasures**, this is broken down into benefits gained at **1st Level**, **5th Level**, and **9th Level**. The benefits are cumulative.

#### **Example Items**

Here are examples illustrating the different treasure types:

**1. Consumable (1st Echelon)**
*   **Name:** Healing Potion
*   **Keywords:** Magic, Potion
*   **Effect:** When you drink this potion as a maneuver, you regain Stamina equal to your recovery value without spending a Recovery.

**2. Trinket (2nd Echelon)**
*   **Name:** Gecko Gloves
*   **Keywords:** Hands, Magic
*   **Effect:** While you wear these gloves, your grip is all but impossible to break. You can’t be disarmed, you can’t lose your grip while climbing unless you are force moved, and any creature grabbed by you takes a bane on the test for the Escape Grab maneuver.

**3. Leveled Treasure (Armor)**
*   **Name:** Grand Scarab
*   **Keywords:** Magic, Medium Armor
*   **1st Level:** While you wear this armor, you gain a +6 bonus to Stamina and you can fly. If you don’t end your turn on the ground, you fall.
*   **5th Level:** The armor’s bonus to Stamina increases to +12. Additionally, you no longer need to end your turn on the ground to avoid falling.
*   **9th Level:** The armor’s bonus to Stamina increases to +21. Additionally, if you fly any distance before making a strike, that strike gains an edge.

### **Understanding Titles**

Titles are narrative rewards earned by heroes for completing significant, heroic deeds. Unlike treasures, they are not physical items but rather acknowledgments of a hero's status and accomplishments that grant them new, permanent abilities or benefits. A hero does not choose a title; they earn it when the Director determines they have met the requirements.

#### **The Data Model of a Title**

When I award a title to the heroes in your campaign, I will present it using the following structure:

*   **Name:** The name of the title (e.g., *Dragon Blooded*).
*   **Flavor Text:** A short quote or description that embodies the title.
*   **Prerequisite:** The specific, narrative achievement required to earn the title. This is the most critical component. It is a story milestone, not something that can be achieved through a simple roll. (e.g., "You defeat a leader or solo creature with the Dragon keyword").
*   **Echelon:** The recommended Echelon at which this title is typically earned.
*   **Effect:** The mechanical benefit the title provides. Often, a title offers a **choice of benefits**, allowing each player to customize their reward. A hero can only choose one benefit from the list provided by a single title.

#### **Example Titles**

Here are examples illustrating how titles function across different echelons:

**1. 1st-Echelon Title**
*   **Name:** Local Hero
*   **Prerequisite:** You save a community from certain destruction.
*   **Effect:** Choose one of the following benefits:
    *   **Easy Marks:** You gain an edge on tests made using skills from the interpersonal and intrigue skill groups when influencing members of a community that you have saved.
    *   **Local Fame:** You earn 1 Renown.
    *   **A New Dawn:** Each time you finish a respite while in a community you have saved, the party gains a hero token.

**2. 2nd-Echelon Title**
*   **Name:** Giant Slayer
*   **Prerequisite:** You defeat a leader or solo creature with the Giant keyword.
*   **Effect:** Choose one of the following benefits:
    *   **Smallfolk Dodge:** Any creature of size 2 or larger takes a bane on strikes against you.
    *   **The Harder They Fall:** You gain a new heroic ability called "The Harder They Fall" that is particularly effective at knocking large creatures prone.
    *   **Up the Beanstalk:** You gain the Climb skill (or an edge if you already have it) and significant advantages when climbing creatures.

**3. 4th-Echelon Title**
*   **Name:** Demigod
*   **Prerequisite:** You have the Godsworn title, and hundreds of worshipful mortals complete a divine ritual in your name.
*   **Effect:** You gain the following benefits:
    *   **Immortal Excellence:** A characteristic of your choice increases by 1 (to a maximum of 6).
    *   **Longevity:** Your natural lifespan doubles.
    *   **Worshippers:** You magically hear prayers directed to you.
    *   **Additionally, choose one:** Acolytes (more followers), Divine Weapons (change damage type to holy/corruption), or Missionaries (more Renown).
